Einstein's Model: From interacting atoms to normal modes

The hamiltonian of atoms in a crystal lattice, atoms displaced from equilibrium:
✦
\(r_i\) \(\rightarrow\) \(r_i\) \(+\) \(\xi_i\)
where
\(\dot{\xi}_i\) velocity, \(\left(\frac{\partial \xi_i}{\partial t}\right)\)
✦ Total Energy
✦
\(H\) \(=\) \(T\) \(+\) \(V\)
where
\(T\) \(=\) \(\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\sum_i m_i \dot{\xi}^2_i\)
✦
\(T\) \(=\) \(1\) \(2\) \(\displaystyle\sum_i\) \(m_i \xi_i^2\)
Expanding potential energy about equilibrium:
✦
\(V\) \(=\) \(V_0\) \(+\) \(1\) \(2\) \(\displaystyle\sum_i\) \(A_{ij} \xi_i \xi_j\)
Neglecting higher-order terms (harmonic approximation), System of coupled harmonic oscillators after diagonalizing the hamiltonian:
✦
\(H\) \(=\) \(\displaystyle\sum_k\) \(\displaystyle \left(\frac{1}{2}p_k^2 \, {\color{#F08FB0}{+}} \, \frac{1}{2}\omega_k^2 q_k^2 \right)\)

Lattice Vibrations

Physical System
Atoms arranged in a crystal lattice, and interact via interatomic forces.
Small Oscillations
Atoms vibrate about equilibrium positions. For small displacements, harmonic approximation.
✦
\(U\) \(\approx\) \(U_0\) \(+\) \(1\) \(2\) \(\displaystyle\sum_i\) \(K_{ij} q_i q_j\)
General Result
System has \(3N\) normal modes of vibration. Each mode behaves like a harmonic oscillaton.
Einstein's Assumptions
All normal modes have the same frequency: \(\omega = \omega_E\).
Modes are treated as independent.

Physical Model and Quantum Input

  • Atoms in a crystal oscillate about equilibrium.
  • Each normal mode is an independent quantum harmonic oscillator.
  • All oscillators have same frequency \(\omega_E\).
A solid can be modeled as a system of (\(3N\)) independent oscillators.
This is the only quantum input.
✦ Energy Levels
✦
\(\epsilon_n\) \(=\) \(\displaystyle \hbar \omega_E \left(n \, {\color{#F08FB0}{+}} \, \frac{1}{2} \right)\)
✦ Energy Levels

\(\beta\) \(=\) \(1\) \(k_BT\)
✦ Harmonic oscillator partition function
✦
\(Z_1\) \(=\) \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty\) \(e^{-\beta \hbar \omega_E (n+1/2)}\)
✦ Harmonic oscillator partition function
✦

  • Independence, factorization
  • No combinatorics required

  • Thermal excitation term

Heat Capacity and Einstein Temperature

The Einstein model treats each atom as an independent quantum harmonic oscillator, leading to the following heat capacity.
✦ Heat capacity definition
✦
\(C_V\) \(=\) \(\Big(\) \(\partial E\) \(\partial T\) \(\Big)_{\color{#6AAEE0}{V}}\)
✦ Einstein temperature definition
✦
\(\theta_E\) \(=\) \(\hbar \omega_E\) \(k_B\)
  • Characteristic temperature scale of the solid
  • Energy scale: \(\hbar \omega_E = k_B \theta_E\)
  • Sets when quantum effects become important
✦ Einstein heat capacity formula
✦
\(C_V\) \(=\) \(3 N k_B\) \(\Big(\) \(\theta_E\) \(T\) \(\Big)^2\) \(e^{\theta_E/T}\) \((e^{\theta_E/T} - 1)^2\)
  • \(T \gg \theta_E\): classical regime (\(C_V \approx 3 N k_B\))
  • \(T \ll \theta_E\): quantum regime (excitations suppressed)

Limiting Behavior

✦ High temperature (\(T \gg \theta_E\))
✦
\(C_V\) \(\to\) \(3Nk_B\)
(Dulong-Petit law)
✦ Low temperature (\(T \ll \theta_E\))
✦
\(C_V\) \(\sim\) \(3Nk_B\) \(\Big(\) \(\theta_E\) \(T\) \(\Big)^2\) \(e^{-\theta_E/T}\)
  • Exponential suppression
  • \(k_B T \ll \hbar \omega_E \implies\) excitations freeze out

Assessment and Limitation

✦ What it gets right:
  • \(C_V \to 0\) as \(T \to 0\) (qualitative trend)
  • Introduces quantization of lattice vibrations
✦ What it gets wrong:
  • Incorrect low-temperature dependence
  • Predicts \(C_V \sim e^{-\theta_E/T}\)
  • Experiments show \(C_V \propto T^3\)

From Einstein to Debye: What needs fixing?

✦ Einstein model assumption:
✦ All Modes Identical
✦
\(\omega = \omega_E\)
✦ Problem:
  • Real solids do not have a single frequency
  • Vibrations occur over a spectrum of frequencies
✦ Correct viewpoint:
  • A solid has \(3N\) normal modes
  • Each mode has its own frequency \(\omega\)
✦ Einstein
✦
\(\displaystyle\sum\) \(\to\) \(3N \times (\text{same } \omega)\)
✦ Reality
✦
\(\displaystyle\sum\) \(\to\) \(\displaystyle\sum_{\text{modes}} \omega\)
Key Idea
Replace single frequency \(\omega_E\) by a distribution of frequencies.
General Result
System has \(3N\) normal modes of vibration. Each mode behaves like a harmonic oscillator.

Introduction to Debye Approximation

Each normal mode behaves like a quantum harmonic oscillator, but now different modes have different frequencies. So instead of summing identical oscillators, we must sum over all modes with different \(\omega\).
✦ Energy sum over modes
✦
\(E\) \(=\) \(\displaystyle\sum_{k,s}\) \(\hbar \omega_{k,s}\) \(e^{\beta \hbar \omega_{k,s}} - 1\)
To make progress, we convert the sum into an integral:
✦ Integral form
✦
\(E\) \(=\) \(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\) \(\sigma(\omega)\) \(\hbar \omega\) \(e^{\beta \hbar \omega} - 1\) \(d\omega\)
Thus the entire problem reduces to determining \(\sigma(\omega)\).
