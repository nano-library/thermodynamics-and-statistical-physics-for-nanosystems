Aspect Bosons Fermions
Spin Particles with integer spin (\(0, 1, 2, \ldots\)) Particles with half-integer spin (\(\frac{1}{2}, \frac{3}{2}, \ldots\))
Quantum States The total wavefunction is symmetric under particle exchange: \[ \phi(r_1, r_2) = +\phi(r_2, r_1) \] The total wavefunction is antisymmetric under particle exchange: \[ \phi(r_1, r_2) = -\phi(r_2, r_1) \]
Occupation number (n) Multiple identical bosons may occupy the same single-particle quantum state without restriction. \[ n_r = 0, 1, 2, \ldots \] Pauli exclusion principle applies: at most one fermion may occupy a given single-particle quantum state. \[ n_r = 0, 1 \]
Examples Photons (quanta of light), phonons (lattice vibrations), plasmons (collective electron oscillations). Conduction electrons in metals at low temperatures, liquid \(^3\)He, and degenerate fermion systems such as neutron stars and white dwarfs.
Total Energy (E) Sum of energies over all occupied single-particle states: \[ E = \displaystyle\sum_r n_r E_r \] Sum of energies over all occupied single-particle states (where \(n_r \le 1\)): \[ E = \displaystyle\sum_r n_r E_r \]
Statistics Follows Bose-Einstein statistics. Particles are indistinguishable and can share the same microstate. Follows Fermi-Dirac statistics. Particles are indistinguishable and obey the Pauli exclusion principle.
Grand Partition Function (Z) \[ Z_{\text{BE}} = \displaystyle\prod_r \frac{1}{1 - e^{-\beta \left(E_r - \mu\right)}} \] \[ Z_{\text{FD}} = \displaystyle\prod_r \left(1 + e^{-\beta \left(E_r - \mu\right)}\right) \]
Both canonical and grand-canonical partition function forms can be used, depending on the exchange.
