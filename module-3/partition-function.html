✦ Problem 1 probability with velocity distribution of the molecule
Problem
Consider a molecule in a ideal gas system \(A\), which is in contact with reservoir \(A^{'}\) that is moving with constant velocity \(V_0\). The system can exchange energy and momentum with the reservoir. Starting from the equilibrium probability distribution \[ P_r \propto e^{-\beta(\epsilon_r - V_0 \cdot P_r)} \] Show that probability with velocity distribution of the molecule is given by \[ P(v) \propto e^{-\frac{1}{2}\beta m (v - v_0)^2} \]
Solution

For a single molecule with velocity \(v_r\), the kinetic energy and momentum are:

\[ \epsilon_r = \frac{1}{2}mv_r^2, \qquad P_r = mv_r \]

Substituting into the equilibrium distribution:

\[ P_r \propto e^{-\beta \left(\frac{1}{2}mv_r^2 - mv_0 v_r\right)} \]

Complete the square in the exponent:

\[ \begin{align} \frac{1}{2}mv_r^2 - mv_0 v_r &= \frac{1}{2}m\left(v_r^2 - 2v_0 v_r\right) \\ &= \frac{1}{2}m\left(v_r - v_0\right)^2 - \frac{1}{2}mv_0^2 \end{align} \]

Therefore:

\[ \begin{align} P_r &\propto e^{-\beta\left[\frac{1}{2}m(v_r - v_0)^2 - \frac{1}{2}mv_0^2\right]} \\ &= e^{-\frac{1}{2}\beta m(v_r - v_0)^2} \cdot e^{+\frac{1}{2}\beta m v_0^2} \end{align} \]

Since \(e^{+\frac{1}{2}\beta m v_0^2}\) is a constant with respect to \(v_r\):

\[ P(v_r) \propto e^{-\frac{1}{2}\beta m (v_r - v_0)^2} \]

Therefore:

\[ P(v_r) \propto e^{-\beta \left[\frac{1}{2} m (v_r - v_0)^2 \right]} \]

This is the Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distribution, centered at the reservoir drift velocity \(v_0\).

✦ Problem 2 partition functions
Problem
Consider a system consists of two particles each of which can occupy one of the 3 quantum states with energies \(0\), \(E\), \(3E\). The system is in contact with a heat reservoir at the temperature \(T\), where \(\beta = \frac{1}{kT}\).
a Write an expression for the partition if the particles are distinguishable
b Write an expression for the partition if the particles are indistinguishable. (Bose-Einstein statistics)
c Write an expression for the partition if the particles are indistinguishable. (Fermi-Dirac statistics)
Solution

(a)

The partition function is defined as:

\[ Z = \sum_r e^{-\beta E_r} \]

For each distinguishable particle:

\[ Z_1 = Z_2 = 1 + e^{-\beta E} + e^{-3\beta E} \]

Since the particles are distinguishable, the total partition function is:

\[ Z = Z_1 \cdot Z_2 \] \[ Z = \left(1 + e^{-\beta E} + e^{-3\beta E}\right)^2 \]

(b)

Let \(n_0,\, n_1,\, n_2\) be the occupation numbers of energy levels \(\epsilon_0 = 0\), \(\epsilon_1 = E\), \(\epsilon_2 = 3E\) respectively, with the constraint:

\[ n_0 + n_1 + n_2 = 2 \]

For bosons, multiple occupancy is allowed. The allowed states \((n_0, n_1, n_2)\) and their total energies are:

\[ \begin{array}{ccc} (n_0,\, n_1,\, n_2) & & E_{\text{total}} \\ \hline (2,\, 0,\, 0) & & 0 \\ (0,\, 2,\, 0) & & 2E \\ (0,\, 0,\, 2) & & 6E \\ (1,\, 1,\, 0) & & E \\ (1,\, 0,\, 1) & & 3E \\ (0,\, 1,\, 1) & & 4E \\ \end{array} \] \[ Z_{\text{BE}} = 1 + e^{-\beta E} + e^{-2\beta E} + e^{-3\beta E} + e^{-4\beta E} + e^{-6\beta E} \]

(c)

For fermions, the Pauli exclusion principle forbids multiple occupancy (\(n_i \leq 1\)). The allowed states \((n_0, n_1, n_2)\) and their total energies are:

\[ \begin{array}{ccc} (n_0,\, n_1,\, n_2) & & E_{\text{total}} \\ \hline (1,\, 1,\, 0) & & E \\ (1,\, 0,\, 1) & & 3E \\ (0,\, 1,\, 1) & & 4E \\ \end{array} \] \[ Z_{\text{FD}} = e^{-\beta E} + e^{-3\beta E} + e^{-4\beta E} \]
✦ Problem 3 partition functions
Problem
Consider 3 particles that can occupy 2 energy levels with energies \(0\) and \(E\). Compute the partition function for Maxwell-Boltzmann, Bose-Einstein, and Fermi-Dirac statistics. The system is in contact with a heat reservoir at temperature \(T\), where \(\beta = \frac{1}{kT}\).
a Write an expression for the partition function if the particles are distinguishable. (Maxwell-Boltzmann statistics)
b Write an expression for the partition function if the particles are indistinguishable. (Bose-Einstein statistics)
c Write an expression for the partition function if the particles are indistinguishable. (Fermi-Dirac statistics)
Solution

(a)

The partition function is defined as:

\[ Z = \sum_r e^{-\beta E_r} \]

For each distinguishable particle with energy levels \(0\) and \(E\):

\[ Z_1 = Z_2 = Z_3 = 1 + e^{-\beta E} \]

Since the particles are distinguishable, the total partition function is:

\[ Z_{\text{MB}} = Z_1 \cdot Z_2 \cdot Z_3 \] \[ Z_{\text{MB}} = \left(1 + e^{-\beta E}\right)^3 \]

(b)

Let \(n_0,\, n_1\) be the occupation numbers of energy levels \(\epsilon_0 = 0\) and \(\epsilon_1 = E\) respectively, with the constraint:

\[ n_0 + n_1 = 3 \]

For bosons, multiple occupancy is allowed. The allowed states \((n_0, n_1)\) and their total energies are:

\[ \begin{array}{ccc} (n_0,\, n_1) & & E_{\text{total}} \\ \hline (3,\, 0) & & 0 \\ (2,\, 1) & & E \\ (1,\, 2) & & 2E \\ (0,\, 3) & & 3E \\ \end{array} \] \[ Z_{\text{BE}} = 1 + e^{-\beta E} + e^{-2\beta E} + e^{-3\beta E} \]

(c)

For fermions, the Pauli exclusion principle requires \(n_i \leq 1\). With only 2 energy levels, at most 2 fermions can be accommodated, so it is impossible to distribute 3 fermions across 2 levels.

\[ Z_{\text{FD}} = 0 \]
